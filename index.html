Supervised Land Use / Land Cover Classification of Hyderabad

This interactive Google Earth Engine application classifies Hyderabad's urban landscape into five land-use/land-cover classes using a supervised Random Forest classifier trained on Sentinel-2 surface reflectance and spectral indices. It lets a visitor compare the Sentinel-2 imagery with the classified map, review classification accuracy, and inspect the area occupied by each class.

Processing Workflow
  • Cloud/cirrus masking of Sentinel-2 using the QA60 band; reflectance scaled by 0.0001.
  • Median composite (Jan–Apr 2025) clipped to the Hyderabad boundary, processed at 10 m.
  • Predictor bands: B2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12, plus derived indices NDVI, NDBI and MNDWI.
  • Training samples digitised for five classes: water, built-up, vegetation, cropland, bare land.
  • Classifier: Random Forest (smileRandomForest), 200 trees, seed 42.
  • Post-classification smoothing with a focal-mode filter to reduce salt-and-pepper noise.

Reading the Classified Map

Built-up land dominates the classified surface at roughly two-thirds of the study area, which is consistent with Hyderabad's dense and largely continuous urban fabric. Cropland forms the second largest class at about a quarter of the area and is concentrated towards the periphery, beyond the built-up core.

Vegetation covers only a small fraction of the study area. Within a boundary this urbanised, tree cover survives mainly in parks, institutional campuses and reservoir margins, and much of it sits below the 10 m pixel size as street trees and small gardens that are absorbed into the surrounding built-up signal. Water bodies account for a similarly small share.

Some confusion remains between bare land and built-up, since dry soil and concrete share broadly similar spectral characteristics in the visible and shortwave infrared. Random Forest was chosen because it handles the high-dimensional band and index stack well and is comparatively resistant to overfitting.

Using the App
  • Turn map layers on/off using the Layers button.
  • Compare the True Color imagery with the LULC classification.
  • Toggle the Study Area Boundary layer to see the extent of Hyderabad.
Research Context

This application has been developed as part of an M.Sc. Geoinformatics research project to demonstrate a supervised land use/land cover classification workflow in Google Earth Engine, using Sentinel-2 imagery and a Random Forest classifier over Hyderabad.
